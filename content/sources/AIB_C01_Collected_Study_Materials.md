## 1. Exam orientation

The certification assesses business decisions about AI investment, value, governance, and adoption. It does not require coding or hands-on AWS implementation experience.

The current beta overview lists 85 questions, 170 minutes, and a USD 50 beta fee. Question types are multiple choice and multiple response. English and Japanese are listed. The standard price is USD 100 before applicable adjustments.

The general guide lists 130 minutes, so use your actual appointment confirmation for the applicable version and duration. Its passing score is scaled to 700 out of 1,000, not a direct raw-percentage threshold.

**Official sources**

- [Certification overview](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/certified-ai-business-strategist/)
- [English exam guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-certification/latest/ai-business-strategist-01/ai-business-strategist-01.html)
- [Launch announcement, September 1, 2026](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/training-and-certification/introducing-aws-certified-ai-business-strategist-built-for-the-people-who-scale-ai/)
- [Before Testing: beta policies and ESL accommodation](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/policies/before-testing/)

The landing page says AWS services knowledge is not assessed, while the guide mentions high-level awareness. Treat services as business context and prioritize the decision skills.

## 6. Public reading collection

These materials can usually be read without course enrollment. They supplement the syllabus rather than replacing it. Read the indicated parts, not necessarily every page. Descriptions are concise summaries; the learning actions are study recommendations.

### R01. What is Artificial Intelligence?

[AWS explanation](https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/artificial-intelligence/)

**Focus:** distinctions among AI, ML, and deep learning; applications and business outcomes.  
**Use:** build a vocabulary foundation. You do not need to memorize the history timeline for this preparation route.

### R02. What is Generative AI?

[AWS explanation](https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/generative-ai/)

**Focus:** content generation, foundation models, use cases, and limitations.  
**Use:** distinguish creating a summary from forecasting a numerical outcome; identify quality requirements for generated content.

### R03. What are AI Agents?

[AWS explanation](https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/ai-agents/)

**Focus:** goal-directed behavior, tool use, and agent workflows.  
**Use:** describe what additional action authority means for permissions, oversight, and business risk. Skip implementation details unless they clarify a concept.

### R04. What is RAG?

[AWS explanation](https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/retrieval-augmented-generation/)

**Focus:** connecting a generative model with relevant external information.  
**Use:** understand why retrieval can help with organization-specific or updated knowledge. Check source quality, freshness, and authorization; retrieval is not a correctness guarantee.

### R05. What is Prompt Engineering?

[AWS explanation](https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/prompt-engineering/)

**Focus:** instructions, context, examples, and desired output.  
**Use:** identify whether poor output is caused by unclear instructions, missing information, or a deeper limitation. Prompt changes do not replace security enforcement or evaluation.

### R06. The Path-to-Value Framework

[AWS article](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/navigating-the-generative-ai-journey-the-path-to-value-framework-from-aws/)

**Focus:** connecting AI activity with measurable outcomes and a path toward delivery.  
**Use:** read for business sequencing and the conditions needed to progress beyond experimentation. Treat this as supporting strategy material, not a separate required certification.

### R07. Getting ROI from Generative AI

[AWS Executive Insights episode and article](https://aws.amazon.com/executive-insights/podcast/getting-roi-from-generative-ai-databricks/)

**Focus:** business impact, implementation, and iterative improvement.  
**Use:** connect revenue, efficiency, and customer outcomes to measurements; separate a claimed benefit from one that has actually been realized.

### R08. Generative AI Is the Answer: What Was the Question?

[AWS Executive Insights discussion and transcript](https://aws.amazon.com/executive-insights/content/generative-ai-is-the-answer-what-was-the-question/)

**Focus:** business objectives, data strategy, and transformation.  
**Use:** read the transcript in short sections. After each, note the business problem, organizational requirement, and decision being discussed.

### R09. AWS Responsible AI

[Official responsible AI hub](https://aws.amazon.com/ai/responsible-ai/)

**Focus:** responsible AI dimensions and the AWS approach.  
**Use:** distinguish fairness, privacy, explainability, transparency, and safety rather than treating them as synonyms. Follow only the relevant linked material.

### R10. Responsible AI: From Principles to Production

[AWS article, June 24, 2025](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/enterprise-strategy/responsible-ai-from-principles-to-production/)

**Focus:** governance and culture, repeatable processes, and technical safeguards.  
**Use:** connect a risk to a control, an owner, and a monitoring process. The useful learning point is how principles become operating practices; commercial claims and statistics are not memorization targets.

### R11. Shared Responsibility Model

[AWS official explanation](https://aws.amazon.com/compliance/shared-responsibility-model/)

**Focus:** how provider and customer responsibilities differ with the services selected.  
**Use:** understand that using a managed service does not remove customer responsibility for data and appropriate use. Skip infrastructure administration detail.

### R12. AWS Cloud Adoption Framework

[Official overview](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/overview-aws-cloud-adoption-framework/welcome.html)

**Focus:** business, people, governance, platform, security, and operations perspectives.

## 7. Offline revision notes

These are independent learning explanations, not extracts from private AWS lessons or official question answers. They are included so you can read something useful without opening every link.

### 7.1 Compare capabilities

| Capability | Typical purpose | Business evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| Deterministic rules | Execute explicit conditions consistently | Correctness against the policy and handling of exceptions |
| Predictive ML | Estimate outcomes from patterns | Relevant errors, value of predictions, and changing conditions |
| Generative AI | Produce drafts, summaries, or other content | Factuality, usefulness, review effort, and appropriate usage |
| Agent workflow | Coordinate actions and tools toward a goal | Task success, authorized actions, reversibility, and oversight |

The same system can combine several capabilities. A document workflow might use extraction to identify fields, deterministic logic to enforce a threshold, and generation to explain the result. Evaluate each part rather than assigning one fashionable label to the whole process.

### 7.2 Compare adaptation methods

| Method | Primary change | Typical consideration |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt improvement | Instructions and supplied context | Cheap to experiment with, but bounded by available context and model ability |
| Retrieval | Information available to the response | Source freshness, retrieval quality, permissions, and evidence |
| Fine-tuning | Model behavior through further training | Appropriate examples, maintenance, evaluation, and whether it addresses the actual gap |

These approaches are not mutually exclusive. Changing instructions cannot ensure that absent facts become available. Adding a knowledge source cannot ensure that the right source is retrieved or interpreted correctly.

### 7.3 Build a credible value case

Define the current workflow and its measured problems. Specify whose work changes and what the desired outcome is. Compare realistic alternatives and include the cost of adoption.

Useful distinctions:

- **Baseline:** the measured starting condition.
- **Target:** the intended improvement.
- **Leading signal:** early evidence that may predict the result.
- **Realized outcome:** the result that has actually occurred.
- **Capacity released:** time available for other work.
- **Cash saved:** expenditure genuinely reduced or avoided.

Avoid counting a time saving twice, once as reduced labor cost and again as additional output, unless the assumptions demonstrate that both benefits are distinct and achievable.

### 7.4 Understand simple financial reasoning

For a defined period:

**ROI = (benefits − costs) / costs**

Illustrative example: annual benefits of USD 90,000 and annual costs of USD 60,000 give a simple ROI of 50%. This is arithmetic for learning, not an AWS question.

State whether setup costs, ongoing usage, review, training, maintenance, and ramp-up are included. Test a downside case with lower adoption or higher review effort. Attractive optimistic arithmetic is weak evidence without credible assumptions.

### 7.5 Match safeguards to the failure

| Concern | Relevant response to evaluate |
|---|---|
| Unsupported claims | Evidence checks, appropriate retrieval, evaluation, and review |
| Unauthorized data exposure | Enforced access controls and incident handling |
| Unequal performance | Relevant subgroup assessment and remediation |
| Unapproved tool use | Clear permitted tools, training, and a review process |
| Excessive agent authority | Scoped permissions, approval requirements, logging, and intervention |
| Declining performance | Monitoring thresholds, investigation, and correction |

No single control resolves all risks. A confidentiality instruction is not permission enforcement. A human reviewer needs enough information, time, and authority to be effective.

### 7.6 Assess readiness before expansion

Readiness concerns the organization around the model. Consider ownership, leadership support, staff capability, data availability, workflow changes, support, and risk management.

A pilot may succeed because it uses clean data and enthusiastic volunteers. Wider deployment may expose inconsistent data, unfamiliar users, cost growth, or support gaps. Expansion should test whether the pilot's assumptions transfer.

Define what justifies scaling, what requires remediation, and what would stop the initiative. Keep a fallback for important operations.

### 7.7 Read scenario questions carefully

Identify the goal, the current stage, the key constraint, and the action requested. “First” concerns sequencing; “best” concerns the fit among alternatives. Evaluate each required choice in multiple-response questions.

Do not turn examples into universal rules such as always preferring a pilot, always preferring governance, or always choosing the lowest price. A good answer addresses the situation described.

## 8. Flexible study method

This collection does not require a fixed daily timetable.

| Available time | Useful action |
|---|---|
| 5 minutes | Read one revision subsection or one part of an official article. |
| 15 minutes | Continue a course segment and write a short English explanation. |
| 30 minutes | Work through targeted official practice and review its answer feedback. |
| A longer block | Combine domain review with new practice, or assess exam pacing. |

**Minimum route through the collection**

1. Read the orientation and official guide.
2. Open the role learning plan if you need foundations.
3. Attempt P01 and record gaps.
4. Use the task map to choose courses and readings.
5. Use domain practice and simulation if your access permits.
6. Recheck every official skill statement before deciding you are ready.

Do not buy several overlapping resources immediately. The confirmed free question set and public readings allow a useful start. Check the current included catalog before paying for a subscription.
